## Research Projects

**Productive Selectivity: Addressing the Quality–Retention Trade-off in Student Group Admissions**

_Senior Thesis_

Selective organizations often prioritize the strongest candidates, yet these individuals may also be the least likely to remain committed. This paper studies the trade-off between candidate quality and retention through an empirical experiment and original theoretical models. I develop a game-theoretic framework and a utility model showing that organizations can improve long-run outcomes by accounting for retention alongside performance, rather than selecting solely on observable talent. While motivated by university student organizations, the framework has broader implications for hiring, admissions, and other competitive selection processes.

**A Fast Ewald Sphere-Corrected Reconstruction Algorithm for Cryogenic Electron Microscopy**

_PACM Independent Work_

Cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM) reconstructs three-dimensional molecular structures from two-dimensional images, but conventional methods become inaccurate at high resolutions due to the curvature of the Ewald sphere. This project develops a computationally efficient reconstruction algorithm that reformulates the problem into a linear system and solves it using implicit matrix operations and conjugate gradient methods. Numerical experiments demonstrate accurate reconstructions while reducing some computational burden of Ewald sphere correction.

**The Relationship Between Conservation-Oriented Tourism and Economic Well-Being: A Study of Ecotourism in Costa Rica**

_Junior Paper_

Does ecotourism improve local economic outcomes? Using census data and tourism records from Costa Rica, I examine whether communities located near protected conservation areas experience lower poverty, unemployment, inequality, or other measures of economic hardship. Contrary to much of the existing literature, I find no statistically significant relationship between ecotourism exposure and local economic well-being, highlighting the importance of context, data limitations, and careful evaluation of sustainable development policies.
